# Beyond Meat (BYND): Q2 2025 Investment Memo

**Source:** Q2 2025 earnings release, dated August 6, 2025 (quarter ended June 28, 2025). All figures are in US$. Figures marked *(derived)* are my own calculations from the release. No market price data was used.

**Recommendation: SELL / avoid the equity.** Revenue is shrinking about 20% a year and the operating loss is widening. The company has $1.2B of debt against $117M of cash, and management is now openly preparing to restructure the balance sheet in a way that would heavily dilute shareholders.

## 1. The quarter versus a year ago

| ($M) | Q2 2025 | Q2 2024 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net revenues | 75.0 | 93.2 | -19.6% |
| Gross profit | 8.6 | 13.7 | -37% *(derived)* |
| Gross margin | 11.5% | 14.7% | -3.2 pts |
| Operating expenses | 47.4 | 47.6 | -0.4% |
| Loss from operations | (38.8) | (33.9) | 4.9 worse |
| Operating margin | -51.8% | -36.4% | -15.4 pts |
| Total other income (expense), net | 5.7 | (0.6) | +6.3 swing |
| Net loss | (33.2) | (34.5) | 1.3 better |
| Net loss per share | (0.43) | (0.53) | |
| Adjusted EBITDA | (26.0) | (23.0) | 3.0 worse |
| Adj. EBITDA % of revenue | -34.7% | -24.7% | -10.0 pts |

The smaller net loss is not a real improvement. It came entirely from a $6.3M swing in other income, which the company attributes mainly to foreign-exchange gains. On every operating measure (gross profit, operating loss, Adjusted EBITDA) the quarter was worse than a year ago. Loss per share also flatters the result: the loss and EPS figures imply roughly 77M shares outstanding against about 65M a year ago, an increase of about 19% *(derived)*.

## 2. Drivers by channel

| Channel ($M) | Q2 2025 | Q2 2024 | Change | Volume | Revenue/lb | Share of the decline *(derived)* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| U.S. retail | 32.9 | 44.9 | -26.7% | -24.2% | -3.2% | ~66% |
| U.S. foodservice | 11.1 | 10.4 | +6.8% | +2.3% | +4.4% | offset |
| Int'l retail | 15.9 | 17.6 | -9.8% | -13.1% | +3.9% | ~9% |
| Int'l foodservice | 15.1 | 20.4 | -25.8% | -21.6% | -5.3% | ~29% |

- **The problem is volume, not price.** Total volume fell 18.9% while revenue per pound fell only 0.9%.
- **U.S. retail accounts for about two-thirds of the $18.2M revenue drop.** The causes are weak category demand and lost points of distribution, and higher trade discounts mean the company is spending more on promotions just to defend shelf space. U.S. retail fell from 48% to 44% of total revenue *(derived)*.
- **International foodservice** fell because quick-service restaurant (QSR) customers bought fewer burgers. The decline is concentrated in a few large accounts.
- **U.S. foodservice is the only channel that grew,** but at $11.1M it is too small to change the overall picture.

## 3. Profitability and one-off items

The release itemises $9.2M of charges. $1.7M sits in cost of goods sold (COGS) for winding down operations in China. The other $7.5M sits in operating expenses: $4.5M of non-routine SG&A, $2.5M of legal costs from the arbitration with a former co-manufacturer, and $0.5M for the partial termination of the headquarters lease.

| Excluding the Q2 2025 charges *(derived)* | Q2 2025 adjusted | Q2 2024 reported |
|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 13.7% | 14.7% |
| Operating expenses | 39.9 (-16%) | 47.6 |
| Operating loss | (29.6), -39.5% margin | (33.9), -36.4% margin |

Even after removing the charges, gross margin fell by about 1 point. Cost per pound rose because lower volume spread fixed costs over fewer units, and inventory provisions and materials costs increased. Real cost cuts of about $7.7M in operating expenses, mainly marketing and selling, only partly made up for the lost gross profit. Adjusted EBITDA, which already excludes these items, got $3.0M worse. One caveat: the release does not disclose any one-off items for Q2 2024, so this comparison adjusts the current quarter only.

## 4. Liquidity and funding

- **Balance sheet:** $117.3M of cash including restricted cash, against $1.2B of total debt. Net debt is about $1.08B, debt is roughly 10x cash, and debt is about 4x annualised revenue of roughly $300M *(derived)*.
- **Cash burn:** in the first half of 2025, operating activities used $59.4M (up 24% year over year) and capital expenditure was $6.4M (up from $2.5M). That is about $66M in six months, or roughly $33M a quarter *(derived)*.
- **Funding:** the cash balance includes a $40M initial draw on the Delayed Draw Term Loan from Unprocessed Foods, LLC. Without that draw, cash would be about $77M. At the current burn rate, reported cash covers roughly 3.5 quarters *(derived)*.
- **Risk factors:** the release explicitly names a possible exchange of the convertible notes for equity, "significant dilution" to existing shareholders, warrants issued to the lender, possible tax liabilities from cancellation-of-indebtedness income, and risk to the company's net operating losses. Management says it is pursuing its goal of "strengthening our balance sheet for the longer term."

## 5. Guidance and restructuring

- **Q3 2025 revenue guidance is $68–73M,** which is 3–9% below Q2 at the midpoint of $70.5M, or about $282M annualised *(derived)*. Management again gives no full-year guidance, citing "elevated" uncertainty. In short, management expects revenue to keep falling.
- **Reduction in force:** about 44 employees, or roughly 6% of the global workforce, which implies about 730 employees in total *(derived)*. One-time cash charges will be $0.8–1.3M. Expected savings over the next 12 months are $5–6M in cash compensation plus $0.5–1.0M in stock-based compensation. Payback is fast, but the cash savings of roughly $1.4M a quarter cover only about 4% of quarterly cash burn *(derived)*.
- **Leadership:** John Boken, a Managing Director in AlixPartners' Turnaround and Restructuring practice, has been appointed interim Chief Transformation Officer, and the AlixPartners engagement has been expanded. Taken together with the debt-exchange language in the risk factors, this is a strong sign that a balance-sheet restructuring is coming.

## 6. Recommendation: SELL / avoid

1. **No operating floor yet.** Revenue is down about 20%, Q3 guidance points lower, and gross margin is shrinking even before one-off charges. The cost cuts are real but are being outrun by falling revenue.
2. **The capital structure works against shareholders.** With about $1.08B of net debt and about 3–4 quarters of cash at the current burn rate, any deleveraging probably means swapping debt for equity on terms set by creditors. Share count is already up about 19% year over year.
3. **Earnings quality is poor.** The only improvement in the headline result came from foreign-exchange gains.

**What would change the view:** a sustained return to volume growth in U.S. retail; gross margin back above 15% excluding one-off charges; quarterly cash burn below about $20M; or published terms for a notes exchange that leave meaningful value for existing shareholders. The full terms of the notes and the credit facility are not in this release, so the 10-Q should be reviewed before taking any position in the debt.
