# Beyond Meat (BYND) Q2 2025 Earnings Recap

Source: revised earnings release dated Aug 8, 2025, quarter ended June 28, 2025. The revision corrects reported SG&A and its knock-on effects. The copy I worked from is narrative only. Its financial statements, channel/volume tables, charges table and non-GAAP reconciliation are blank. Everything below comes from the narrative text. "Derived" marks my own arithmetic.

**Takeaway:** Revenue fell 19.6% on volume (-18.9%), driven by U.S. retail and international foodservice. Gross margin slipped to 11.5%. The net loss narrowed only because of $5.7M of other income, mostly FX gains. The operating loss widened. Management gave a Q3 revenue outlook only, with no full-year guidance, and is cutting costs further.

## Results vs. year-ago quarter

| $M except per share | Q2 2025 | Q2 2024 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net revenues | 75.0 | 93.2 | -19.6% |
| Gross profit | 8.6 | 13.7 | -37.2% (derived) |
| Gross margin | 11.5% | 14.7% | -3.2 pts (derived) |
| Operating expenses | 43.5 | 47.6 | -8.6% (derived) |
| Loss from operations | (34.9) | (33.9) | loss $1.0M wider |
| Operating margin | -46.6% | -36.4% | -10.2 pts (derived) |
| Total other income (expense), net | 5.7 | (0.6) | +6.3 swing (derived) |
| Net loss | (29.2) | (34.5) | $5.3M narrower |
| Net loss per share | (0.38) | (0.53) | $0.15 narrower |
| Adjusted EBITDA | (22.1) | (23.0) | $0.9M better |
| Adj. EBITDA margin | -29.5% | -24.7% | -4.8 pts (derived) |

Revenue drivers: volume -18.9% and net revenue per pound -0.9%. Per-pound pricing was hurt by higher trade discounts and mix. FX and price increases partly offset this.

## Channel revenue

| Channel | Q2 2025 ($M) | Q2 2024 ($M) | YoY | Volume | Net rev/lb |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| U.S. retail | 32.9 | 44.9 | -26.7% | -24.2% | -3.2% |
| U.S. foodservice | 11.1 | 10.4 | +6.8% | +2.3% | +4.4% |
| International retail | 15.9 | 17.6 | -9.8% | -13.1% | +3.9% |
| International foodservice | 15.1 | 20.4 | -25.8% | -21.6% | -5.3% |
| Total | 75.0 | 93.2 | -19.6% | -18.9% | -0.9% |

- U.S. retail suffered from weak category demand and fewer points of distribution. Ingredient sales within it were $0.1M, against $0.8M a year ago.
- U.S. foodservice was the one growing channel, helped by ground beef and dinner sausage.
- International retail fell on Canada (burger, sausage, ground) and EU burger. Per-pound pricing rose on FX.
- International foodservice fell on lower burger sales to certain QSR customers.

## Charges in the quarter and underlying figures

| Item | $M | Where recorded |
|---|---|---|
| China suspension/cessation | 1.7 | Cost of goods sold |
| Incremental arbitration legal expense (former co-manufacturer) | 2.5 | Operating expenses |
| Non-routine SG&A | 0.6 | Operating expenses |
| Partial lease termination, El Segundo HQ | 0.5 | Operating expenses |
| Total | 5.3 | Matches the release's net-loss aggregate |

Derived underlying figures (not company-reported):
- Gross profit before the China charge: $10.3M, or 13.7% margin.
- Operating loss before all four items: about $29.6M, or -39.5% of revenue.
- Net loss before the $5.3M: about $23.9M, or roughly $0.31 per share. This scales the reported $0.38 and is approximate.
- Excluding the charges, operating expenses were about $39.9M, down about 16% from $47.6M. The release credits lower marketing and selling spend.

Gross margin was also hurt by higher cost per pound (inventory provision, materials, lower volume), partly offset by lower manufacturing (including depreciation) and logistics costs. The net loss benefited from FX transaction gains in other income.

## Cash flow and balance sheet

- Cash including restricted cash was $117.3M. Total debt was $1.2B.
- Six-month operating cash flow was -$59.4M, against -$47.8M a year ago. Capex was $6.4M ($2.5M a year ago).
- Investing cash flow was -$6.1M, against +$1.2M a year ago.
- Financing cash flow was +$33.6M, against -$1.0M a year ago. It included an initial $40.0M draw on the Delayed Draw Term Loan with Unprocessed Foods, LLC, net of issuance costs.
- Derived: first-half free cash flow (operating cash flow less capex) was about -$65.8M. Net debt was about $1.08B. Cash of $117.3M covers roughly two more years at the first-half operating rate of about $59M per half, before debt service or the Q3 restructuring costs.
- The release points to dilution risk from convertible note exchanges and from warrants under the loan agreement. It also lists note refinancing and repurchase as risks.

## Guidance and cost actions

- **Outlook:** Q3 2025 net revenues of $68M to $73M. No full-year guidance. Derived: the midpoint of $70.5M is about 6% below Q2's $75.0M. The release has no year-ago Q3 figure to compare it with.
- **Reduction in force (approved Aug 6):** about 44 North American employees, around 6% of the global workforce (implies roughly 730 total, derived).
  - One-time cash charges are $0.8M to $1.3M, mostly in Q3.
  - Expected savings over twelve months are $5.0M to $6.0M in cash compensation, plus $0.5M to $1.0M of non-cash stock-based compensation.
  - Derived: the cash payback is about 2 to 3 months.
- **Leadership and strategy:** John Boken of AlixPartners is interim Chief Transformation Officer. The AlixPartners relationship is expanding. Management says it will cut operating expenses faster, prioritize distribution of core lines, and invest in margin expansion on core products.

## Derived observations

- U.S. retail (-$12.0M) and international foodservice (-$5.3M) account for about 95% of the $18.2M revenue decline. U.S. foodservice added $0.7M. Channel figures are rounded, so they do not sum exactly.
- U.S. retail is 43.9% of Q2 revenue, down from 48.2% a year ago.
- The $1.0M wider operating loss was a $5.1M gross profit decline, offset by $4.1M of lower operating expenses, even after $3.6M of opex charges.
- The headline net loss improvement of $5.3M is smaller than the $6.3M swing in other income, so operations did not improve below the line.
- Adjusted EBITDA improved by $0.9M even though margin fell, as revenue shrank faster than losses.
