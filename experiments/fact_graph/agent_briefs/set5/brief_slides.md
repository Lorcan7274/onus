# Beyond Meat (BYND) Q2 2025 Results: Deck Outline

Source: Beyond Meat Exhibit 99.1 earnings release dated August 6, 2025 (quarter ended June 28, 2025). Figures in $ millions unless noted. "Derived" figures are my calculations from rounded release figures.

## Slide 1: Q2 2025 at a glance

- Net revenues fell 19.6% year over year to $75.0M (from $93.2M), driven by an 18.9% drop in volume
- Gross margin declined to 11.5% from 14.7%, including $1.7M of China-related charges
- Adjusted EBITDA loss widened to $26.0M (-34.7% of revenue) from $23.0M (-24.7%)
- Net loss narrowed to $33.2M ($0.43 per share) from $34.5M ($0.53), helped by $5.7M of other income
- Management is accelerating cost reduction and transformation; no full-year guidance, Q3 revenue outlook of $68M to $73M

## Slide 2: Results versus year-ago quarter

| Metric | Q2 2025 | Q2 2024 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net revenues | $75.0M | $93.2M | -19.6% |
| Gross profit | $8.6M | $13.7M | -37.2% (derived) |
| Gross margin | 11.5% | 14.7% | -3.2 pts |
| Operating expenses | $47.4M | $47.6M | -0.4% |
| Loss from operations | $(38.8)M | $(33.9)M | $4.9M larger loss |
| Operating margin | -51.8% | -36.4% | -15.4 pts |
| Other income (expense), net | $5.7M income | $(0.6)M expense | +$6.3M |
| Net loss | $(33.2)M | $(34.5)M | $1.3M smaller loss |
| Net loss per share | $(0.43) | $(0.53) | +$0.10 |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $(26.0)M | $(23.0)M | -34.7% vs. -24.7% of revenue |

- Volume of products sold fell 18.9%; net revenue per pound fell 0.9%
- Operating loss grew even as operating expenses were flat, because gross profit fell more than opex

## Slide 3: Channel performance

| Channel | Q2 2025 | Q2 2024 | Change | Volume | Revenue per lb |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| U.S. retail | $32.9M | $44.9M | -26.7% | -24.2% | -3.2% |
| U.S. foodservice | $11.1M | $10.4M | +6.8% | +2.3% | +4.4% |
| International retail | $15.9M | $17.6M | -9.8% | -13.1% | +3.9% |
| International foodservice | $15.1M | $20.4M | -25.8% | -21.6% | -5.3% |

- U.S. retail is the largest drag: weak category demand and fewer points of distribution, plus higher trade discounts
- U.S. foodservice is the only channel that grew, on ground beef and dinner sausage volume and price increases
- International foodservice decline reflects lower burger sales to certain QSR customers
- International retail declines were concentrated in Canada (burger, dinner sausage, ground beef) and the EU (burger)
- U.S. retail ingredient sales were $0.1M, down from $0.8M a year earlier

## Slide 4: Costs, margins and one-time items

- Gross margin pressure came from higher inventory provision and higher materials expense, partly offset by lower manufacturing (including depreciation) and logistics expense
- Identified one-time items: $1.7M China wind-down in cost of goods sold; $4.5M non-routine SG&A; $2.5M arbitration legal costs with a former co-manufacturer; $0.5M partial lease termination at Campus Headquarters
- These items total $9.2M, which the release cites as the aggregate effect on net loss
- Operating expenses excluding the $7.5M of identified opex items fell, driven by lower marketing and selling expense
- Reduction in force: about 44 North American employees (~6% of global workforce); one-time cash charges of $0.8M to $1.3M, mostly in Q3 2025
- RIF expected to save $5.0M to $6.0M in cash compensation over 12 months, plus $0.5M to $1.0M in non-cash stock compensation savings

## Slide 5: Cash, debt and financing

- Cash and cash equivalents (including restricted cash): $117.3M at June 28, 2025
- Total outstanding debt: $1.2B
- Six-month operating cash outflow widened to $59.4M from $47.8M
- Capital expenditures rose to $6.4M from $2.5M
- Investing cash flow was a $6.1M outflow, versus a $1.2M inflow a year earlier
- Financing inflow of $33.6M includes an initial $40.0M draw on the Delayed Draw Term Loan with Unprocessed Foods, LLC, net of issuance costs
- Risk factors flag the convertible notes (repurchase, conversion and exchange dilution), the Loan and Security Agreement (dated May 7, 2025, with warrants), and reliance on an "at the market" equity program

## Slide 6: Outlook and strategic response

- No full-year 2025 guidance; management cites elevated operating uncertainty
- Q3 2025 net revenue expected at $68M to $73M
- Actions: accelerate transformation, cut operating expenses faster to match near-term revenue, prioritize distribution of core product lines, and invest in margin expansion on core products
- Expanded AlixPartners relationship; John Boken appointed interim Chief Transformation Officer (August 6, 2025)
- Stated longer-term goal: strengthen the balance sheet
- Watch items named in the release: Campus Headquarters lease obligations and sublease progress, further impairment or inventory write-downs, and QSR partnership timing

## Slide 7: Our derived figures

- Gross profit excluding the $1.7M China charge: $10.3M, a 13.7% margin (versus 14.7% a year earlier; year-ago China charges are not disclosed, so this comparison is approximate)
- Operating expenses excluding the $7.5M of identified items: $39.9M, down 16.2% from $47.6M (assumes no comparable items in the year-ago quarter)
- Revenue mix: U.S. total $44.0M vs. $55.3M (-20.4%); International total $31.0M vs. $38.0M (-18.4%). Foodservice share rose to 34.9% of revenue from 33.0%
- Implied weighted share count: about 77M (33.2 / 0.43) versus about 65M (34.5 / 0.53), roughly +19%, sensitive to EPS rounding
- Six-month operating cash outflow plus capex: $65.8M, versus $50.3M a year earlier (+31%). Annualized, that is about $132M against $117.3M of cash at quarter end, which is illustrative only and not a forecast
- Q3 outlook midpoint of $70.5M implies about -6.0% sequential change from Q2 (range -9.3% to -2.7%)
- Cash RIF payback: midpoint charge of $1.05M against midpoint cash savings of $5.5M implies roughly 2.3 months of savings

## Slide 8: Caveats and open questions

- Source is the press release only; the channel and volume tables in the file are blank, so channel figures come from the narrative text
- Percentages in the release are computed on unrounded figures, so some of my recalculations differ by 0.1 points (for example, the release's -19.6% revenue decline versus -19.5% from rounded figures)
- Derived per-share, headcount and annualized cash figures rely on rounded inputs and are approximate
- Year-ago one-time items and Q3 2024 comparisons are not in this document; a full model needs the 10-Q and the earnings call transcript
- Open questions for management: pace and size of opex cuts beyond the RIF, inventory provision trajectory, financing needs beyond the $40.0M draw, and status of the convertible note exchange and repurchase options
