# Beyond Meat (BYND) Q2 2025 Earnings Brief

*Quarter ended June 28, 2025; reported August 6, 2025. Source: company earnings release only. Figures marked "derived" are my calculations.*

## Headline

Revenue fell 19.6% to $75.0M on weak U.S. retail demand and lost international QSR burger volume. Gross margin slipped to 11.5%, and the operating loss widened to $38.8M (-51.8% margin). The smaller net loss ($33.2M) came from a $6.3M swing in other income (mostly FX gains), not from better operations. Management is cutting costs faster (6% reduction in force, an AlixPartners-led transformation) and guided Q3 revenue to $68-73M, which implies another sequential decline. The balance sheet is the main issue: $117.3M of cash against $1.2B of debt, with H1 operating cash burn of $59.4M.

## Key numbers vs. year-ago quarter

| Metric | Q2 2025 | Q2 2024 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net revenue | $75.0M | $93.2M | -19.6% (-$18.2M) |
| Gross profit | $8.6M | $13.7M | -$5.1M (about -37%) |
| Gross margin | 11.5% | 14.7% | -3.2 pts |
| Operating expenses | $47.4M | $47.6M | about flat (-$0.2M) |
| Operating loss | $(38.8)M | $(33.9)M | loss +$4.9M (about +14%) |
| Operating margin | -51.8% | -36.4% | -15.4 pts |
| Net loss | $(33.2)M | $(34.5)M | loss -$1.3M |
| EPS | $(0.43) | $(0.53) | +$0.10 better |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $(26.0)M | $(23.0)M | loss +$3.0M |
| Adj. EBITDA margin | -34.7% | -24.7% | -10.0 pts |

- **Revenue drivers:** Volume fell 18.9%. Net revenue per pound fell 0.9% on higher trade discounts and mix, partly offset by FX and price increases.
- **Gross margin:** The $1.7M of China wind-down costs sit in COGS. Higher cost per pound (inventory provisions, materials, lower volume) was partly offset by lower manufacturing and logistics expense.
- **Opex:** The quarter includes $4.5M of non-routine SG&A, $2.5M of incremental arbitration legal costs (former co-manufacturer dispute) and $0.5M of lease termination costs. Underlying opex fell on lower marketing and selling spend.
- **Net loss bridge:** Other income was +$5.7M versus -$0.6M a year ago, mainly FX transaction gains.

## Channel trends

| Channel | Q2 2025 | Q2 2024 | YoY | Volume | Net rev/lb |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| U.S. retail | $32.9M | $44.9M | -26.7% | -24.2% | -3.2% |
| U.S. foodservice | $11.1M | $10.4M | +6.8% | +2.3% | +4.4% |
| Int'l retail | $15.9M | $17.6M | -9.8% | -13.1% | +3.9% |
| Int'l foodservice | $15.1M | $20.4M | -25.8% | -21.6% | -5.3% |

- **U.S. retail** is the core problem. It is about 44% of revenue (derived) and contributed roughly $12M of the $18.2M total decline (derived). Causes are weak category demand, fewer points of distribution and heavier trade discounts.
- **U.S. foodservice** is the only growing channel, on ground beef and dinner sausage plus pricing.
- **International retail** declined on lower Canada sales (burger, dinner sausage, ground beef) and EU burgers. FX helped price/mix.
- **International foodservice** declined on lower burger sales to certain QSR customers.
- **Geography (derived):** U.S. revenue was about $44.0M versus $55.3M (-20%). International was about $31.0M versus $38.0M (-18%).

## Cash and debt

- Cash, including restricted cash, was $117.3M. Total debt was about $1.2B as of June 28, 2025.
- H1 operating cash burn was $59.4M (vs. $47.8M). Capex was $6.4M (vs. $2.5M). Derived H1 free cash flow was about -$65.8M versus -$50.3M.
- Financing inflow in H1 was $33.6M, including an initial $40.0M draw on the Unprocessed Foods delayed draw term loan.
- **Runway (derived):** At the H1 average burn of about $33M per quarter (operating cash flow plus capex), $117.3M covers roughly 3.5 quarters before any further financing, and less if burn rises or the remaining facility is not available. The release's risk factors flag sufficiency of cash, refinancing the convertible notes, and significant dilution from note exchanges and lender warrants.

## Guidance

- No full-year guidance. The only outlook is Q3 2025 net revenue of $68-73M.
- Derived: the midpoint of $70.5M is about 6% below Q2's $75.0M, and the range spans -9% to -3% sequentially. The release does not give the Q3 2024 base, so I do not state a year-over-year figure.
- Cost actions: a reduction in force of about 44 North American employees (about 6% of global headcount). One-time cash cost is $0.8-1.3M, mostly in Q3. Expected savings over 12 months are $5-6M cash and $0.5-1.0M non-cash stock comp.
- John Boken (AlixPartners) was named interim Chief Transformation Officer. The priorities are faster opex cuts, wider distribution of core products and margin expansion.

## Analysis

- **Quality of the net loss improvement is low.** The company-disclosed one-time items total $9.2M ($1.7M COGS plus $7.5M opex). Excluding them, the operating loss would be about $29.6M, or -39.5% of revenue (derived). That is still worse than the reported -36.4% a year ago. The net loss beat depends on non-operating FX gains.
- **Operating deleverage.** Revenue fell $18.2M, gross profit fell $5.1M (about 28% of the revenue drop), and opex was flat. Excluding the $7.5M of flagged opex items, underlying opex was about $39.9M, roughly 16% below the year-ago $47.6M (derived, assuming no comparable items last year, which the release does not state). Cost cuts are real but not yet keeping pace with the revenue decline.
- **Gross margin ex-China** would be about 13.7% (($8.6M + $1.7M) / $75.0M, derived), against 14.7% last year. The underlying decline is about 1 point, so the margin pressure is mostly volume and inventory provisions rather than only the China charge.
- **Run-rate.** The Q2 operating loss annualizes to about $155M and adjusted EBITDA to about -$104M (derived), against a $117M cash balance. A Q3 revenue midpoint of $70.5M with gross margin near 11-14% implies gross profit of only about $8-10M against opex of $40-47M (derived estimate), so burn is unlikely to ease soon. RIF savings of about $5-6M a year are small relative to the loss.
- **What to watch:** (1) U.S. retail volume and distribution points, (2) gross margin ex-one-time items, (3) opex trajectory toward revenue, (4) financing actions on the convertible notes and the term loan, including dilution, and (5) whether Q3 lands inside the $68-73M range.
- **Bottom line:** Operations are deteriorating on weak category demand, and the capital structure leaves little room for error. The position depends on restructuring execution and balance sheet actions more than on a demand recovery.
