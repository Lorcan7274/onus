# Beyond Meat (BYND): Q2 2025 Results Note

**Quarter ended June 28, 2025 | Released August 6, 2025**
**Source:** Company Q2 2025 earnings release (narrative text only; the release's tables were not in the source file). Figures marked *derived* are analyst calculations from release figures and may differ slightly because of rounding.

---

## Investment summary

- **Demand is still falling, and cost cuts are not keeping up.** Net revenues fell 19.6% to $75.0m. Volume was down 18.9% and net revenue per pound down 0.9%. U.S. retail (-26.7%) and international foodservice (-25.8%) drove about 95% of the $18.2m decline (*derived*).
- **Most of the margin damage is real, not one-off.** Reported gross margin fell 3.2pts to 11.5%. Excluding the $1.7m China charge, underlying gross margin was about 13.7% (*derived*), still 1.0pt below the year-ago quarter.
- **Opex discipline is visible underneath the charges.** Reported opex was flat at $47.4m. Excluding $7.5m of non-routine items, underlying opex was about $39.9m, down roughly 16% YoY (*derived*).
- **The balance sheet is the central issue.** Cash (including restricted cash) was $117.3m against $1.2bn of debt. H1 free cash outflow was about $65.8m (*derived*), and the company relied on a $40.0m term-loan draw. On H1 run-rate burn, runway is roughly 3.5 quarters without new capital (*derived*).
- **Q3 guidance points to further decline:** $68m–$73m, which is 3%–9% below Q2 (*derived*). There is no full-year guidance.
- **View:** this is now a restructuring story. Key watch items are the pace of cost removal (a 6% RIF; AlixPartners' John Boken is interim Chief Transformation Officer) and, above all, the outcome of efforts to "strengthen our balance sheet."

- **U.S. retail** accounts for about two-thirds of the revenue decline (-$12.0m, *derived*). Causes are weak category demand, lost distribution points and higher trade discounts. Ingredient sales fell to $0.1m from $0.8m, so the core retail decline was about -25.6% (*derived*).

- **International retail** held price (+3.9%/lb, partly FX), but volume fell in Canada and EU burgers.

**Our read:**
1. **Gross margin.** Reported COGS fell 16.5% while volume fell 18.9%, so COGS per pound rose about 3% (*derived*). Excluding the China charge, COGS per pound was roughly flat. The underlying 1.0pt margin decline therefore comes mainly from lower revenue per pound (trade spend and mix), a higher inventory provision and higher materials costs. Lower manufacturing and logistics costs only partly offset these.
2. **Opex.** Underlying opex fell about $7.7m YoY, mostly from lower marketing and selling spend. This is real progress. However, cutting marketing during a demand decline risks deepening the volume problem.
3. **Below the line.** The $24.0m ex-charges net loss includes a $5.7m other-income gain, mostly FX, which should not be capitalised. Excluding it as well, the underlying net loss is closer to about $30m (*derived*).
4. **Adjusted EBITDA** worsened by $3.0m on $18.2m less revenue, a decremental of about 16% (*derived*). The cost base is not yet variable enough.

- **Cash including restricted cash: $117.3m. Total debt: about $1.2bn.** Net debt is about $1.08bn (*derived*), roughly 3.6x annualised Q2 revenue of $300m (*derived*).
- **Burn and runway (*derived*).** H1 free cash outflow averaged about $33m per quarter, so $117.3m covers roughly 3.5 quarters, into mid-2026, before any new funding. Q3 RIF severance of $0.8m–$1.3m shortens this slightly. Without the $40.0m Unprocessed Foods term-loan draw, period-end cash would have been about $77m.
- **RIF savings are small relative to the burn.** Expected cash savings are $5m–$6m over 12 months, about $1.3m–$1.5m per quarter, or only about 4%–5% of the current quarterly burn (*derived*). The payback on severance is quick (about 2–3 months), but this will not close the gap on its own.
- **Capital structure risk.** The release's risk factors cite refinancing of the convertible notes, possible exchanges of notes for equity (significant dilution), warrants attached to the term loan, and possible cancellation-of-debt tax liabilities. The amount drawn on the term loan, any undrawn capacity, and note maturities are not given in the source.

Guidance implies a further sequential decline in a quarter that normally includes part of the grilling season. This means cash burn is unlikely to improve materially in Q3.

## What matters next (no valuation view)

1. **Balance sheet action.** Management says it is still pursuing "strengthening our balance sheet." Watch the terms of any convertible-note exchange or refinancing, the resulting dilution, and further use of the term-loan facility. This will drive the equity outcome more than operations will.
2. **Burn trajectory.** Operating cash outflow plus capex needs to fall well below about $33m per quarter. Watch whether AlixPartners-led measures go beyond the 44-person RIF, for example manufacturing footprint, the HQ lease and further COGS work.
3. **Distribution in U.S. retail.** Lost points of distribution are harder to win back than lost velocity. The company's stated priority is increasing distribution of core lines; shelf-reset outcomes will show whether that is working.
4. **QSR exposure in international foodservice.** Watch whether the lost burger volume with certain QSR customers stabilises or worsens.
5. **Underlying gross margin.** Watch whether inventory provisions recede and pricing holds against trade spend. A sustained return to mid-teens gross margin or better is a precondition for any credible path toward breakeven.
6. **Remaining one-offs.** These include the arbitration outcome and ongoing legal costs, the China exit tail, and further HQ lease actions. Each is small on its own, but together they totalled $9.2m this quarter.
