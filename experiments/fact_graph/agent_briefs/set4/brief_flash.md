# Beyond Meat (BYND): Q2 2025 Earnings Flash, First Take

*Source: Q2 2025 earnings release dated August 6, 2025 (quarter ended June 28, 2025). No other sources. Derived figures are my calculations and may differ by rounding. In the source file the channel, volume and charge tables contain labels only, so those figures come from the narrative text.*

## Bottom line

Revenue fell 19.6% on an 18.9% volume decline, and gross margin fell 320 bps to 11.5%. Reported opex was flat, but it was down about 16% excluding $7.5M of named items. The operating loss widened to $38.8M. The net loss narrowed by $1.3M only because other income swung $6.3M to a $5.7M gain, which the release attributes mainly to FX. Adjusted EBITDA worsened by $3.0M. There is no full-year guidance. Q3 revenue is guided to $68M to $73M.

## Headline numbers vs. year-ago quarter

- **Net revenue:** $75.0M, down 19.6% (from $93.2M)
- **Gross margin:** 11.5%, down 320 bps (from 14.7%). Excluding the $1.7M China charge, 13.7%.
- **Operating loss:** $38.8M, an operating margin of -51.8% (from -$33.9M, -36.4%)
- **Net loss:** $33.2M, or $(0.43) per share (from $34.5M, or $(0.53))
- **Adjusted EBITDA:** $(26.0)M, or -34.7% of revenue (from $(23.0)M, or -24.7%)

- **U.S. retail is most of the problem.** It accounts for about two-thirds of the $18.2M revenue decline. Weak category demand and fewer points of distribution drove volume down. Higher trade discounts pushed net price per lb down. Excluding ingredient sales ($0.1M vs. $0.8M), core U.S. retail fell about 25.6%.
- **U.S. foodservice is the only growing channel,** led by ground beef and dinner sausage, with price increases helping.
- **International foodservice** had the steepest percentage decline, from lower QSR burger volume.
- **International retail** weakened in Canada and the EU (burgers, dinner sausage, ground beef). FX and price helped per-lb revenue.

## Gross margin and costs

- Gross profit fell $5.1M. Excluding the $1.7M China charge, underlying gross profit was $10.3M vs. $13.7M. My rough split: about $2.7M from lower volume and about $0.7M from a lower margin rate.
- Cost of goods per lb rose on higher inventory provisions and materials costs, partly offset by lower manufacturing (including depreciation) and logistics costs.
- Q2 2025 charges total $9.2M: China $1.7M, non-routine SG&A $4.5M, arbitration $2.5M and lease termination $0.5M. Excluding these, the adjusted operating loss is about $29.6M (about -39.5% of revenue). This is my calculation, not a company non-GAAP figure.

- **Burn and runway:** H1 free cash flow averaged about -$33M per quarter. At that pace, $117M of cash lasts roughly 3.5 quarters, or into early 2026, before any new financing. The release mentions an "at the market" program but gives no usage data.
- **Leverage:** Debt is about 10x cash and about 4x annualized Q2 revenue ($300M). Interest cost is not in the file.

## Guidance and cost actions

- **Guidance:** No full-year outlook. Q3 2025 revenue is guided to $68M to $73M. That is -2.7% to -9.3% sequentially (midpoint $70.5M, -6.0%), or about $272M to $292M annualized.
- **Reduction in force:** About 44 North American employees (about 6% of the global workforce, implying roughly 730 total). One-time cash charges are $0.8M to $1.3M, mostly in Q3, though timing could slip. Expected savings are $5.0M to $6.0M of cash compensation over 12 months (about $1.25M to $1.5M per quarter) plus $0.5M to $1.0M non-cash. That is about 3% to 4% of the annualized ex-item opex run-rate of about $160M, and small against a roughly $33M quarterly cash burn.
- **Other actions:** John Boken (AlixPartners) was named interim Chief Transformation Officer and the AlixPartners engagement was expanded. Campus HQ was partially lease-terminated, and further surrender or sublease options are open, with possible impairment. Operations in China were suspended and largely ceased in H1 2025.

## Three key questions for management

1. **Where does the cost base settle?** Ex-item opex fell to $39.9M, but the RIF saves only $5M to $6M a year. What quarterly opex and gross margin level gets Adjusted EBITDA to breakeven at $68M to $73M of quarterly revenue, and by when?
2. **Is U.S. retail volume a distribution problem or a demand problem?** Volume fell 24% and trade discounts cut net price per lb by 3.2%. How many points of distribution were lost, how much is recoverable at the Q3 shelf reset, and how much of the price erosion is structural?
3. **How does the balance sheet get fixed, and at what dilution?** With $117M of cash, about $66M of H1 free cash flow burn and about $1.2B of debt, what is the sequencing of note exchanges, warrant exercises, at-the-market sales and refinancing? Are further draws available from the Unprocessed Foods facility, and what is the minimum cash level management is managing to?
