# Beyond Meat (BYND) - Q2 2025 One-Pager

Source: revised earnings release dated Aug 8, 2025 (quarter ended Jun 28, 2025). The revision corrects only SG&A and its knock-on effects. The file I read is narrative only: the channel, volume, charge and non-GAAP tables are missing, so everything below comes from the text. "Derived" marks my own arithmetic.

## Results vs. year-ago quarter

| $M except per share | Q2 25 | Q2 24 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net revenues | 75.0 | 93.2 | -19.6% (reported) |
| Gross profit | 8.6 | 13.7 | -37% (derived) |
| Gross margin | 11.5% | 14.7% | -3.2 pts (derived) |
| Operating expenses | 43.5 | 47.6 | -8.6% (derived) |
| Loss from operations | (34.9) | (33.9) | loss up 2.9% (derived) |
| Operating margin | -46.6% | -36.4% | -10.2 pts (derived) |
| Net loss | (29.2) | (34.5) | loss down 15.4% (derived) |
| Net loss per share | (0.38) | (0.53) | loss down 28% (derived) |
| Adjusted EBITDA | (22.1) | (23.0) | loss down $0.9M |
| Adj. EBITDA margin | -29.5% | -24.7% | -4.8 pts (derived) |

- Revenue fell on volume: volume -18.9%, net revenue per pound -0.9%. Lower per-pound pricing came from higher trade discounts and mix, partly offset by FX and price increases.
- The core business got worse. The operating loss widened and gross margin fell. The smaller net loss came from other income of +$5.7M, against -$0.6M a year ago, mostly FX transaction gains. That is about $6.3M of swing (derived) and probably not repeatable.
- Operating expenses fell, but they were 58% of revenue against 51% a year ago (derived).
- Gross margin fell on higher cost per pound: higher inventory provision and materials costs, and fixed-cost deleverage from lower volume. Lower manufacturing and logistics costs partly offset these.

## Drivers by channel

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| U.S. retail | 32.9 | 44.9 | -26.7% | -24.2% / -3.2% | 44% (was 48%) |
| U.S. foodservice | 11.1 | 10.4 | +6.8% | +2.3% / +4.4% | 15% (was 11%) |
| Int'l retail | 15.9 | 17.6 | -9.8% | -13.1% / +3.9% | 21% (was 19%) |
| Int'l foodservice | 15.1 | 20.4 | -25.8% | -21.6% / -5.3% | 20% (was 22%) |

- U.S. retail was the main problem. Causes were weak category demand, fewer points of distribution and higher trade discounts. Ingredient sales were $0.1M, against $0.8M a year ago.
- International foodservice fell on lower burger sales to certain QSR customers.
- U.S. foodservice was the bright spot, helped by ground beef and dinner sausage and by pricing.
- International retail fell on lower sales in Canada and burger sales in the EU. Favorable FX and pricing helped per-pound revenue.
- Derived: U.S. retail accounts for about two-thirds of the roughly $18.2M revenue decline, and international foodservice for about 29%. Foodservice growth in the U.S. offset only about $0.7M.
- Channel figures sum to $75.0M. The year-ago channels sum to $93.3M because of rounding.

## Charges in the quarter

- Cost of goods sold: $1.7M for the suspension and substantial cessation of China operations.
- Operating expenses: $2.5M incremental arbitration legal costs (dispute with a former co-manufacturer), $0.6M non-routine SG&A, and $0.5M for the partial termination of the El Segundo HQ lease. That is $3.6M in total (derived).
- Aggregate charges: $5.3M, which is 18% of the net loss (derived).
- Derived, ex-charges: gross margin would be about 13.7% ($10.3M), and operating loss about $29.6M, or about -39.5% of revenue. These are not company-reported adjusted figures.

## Cash and debt (June 28, 2025)

- Cash, including restricted cash: $117.3M. Total debt: about $1.2B. Derived net debt is about $1.08B, and cash covers about 10% of debt.
- Six-month operating cash use was $59.4M (vs. $47.8M, +24%). Capex was $6.4M (vs. $2.5M). Investing cash use was $6.1M.
- Financing provided $33.6M. It included an initial $40.0M draw on the Unprocessed Foods delayed draw term loan, less debt issuance costs.
- Derived free cash flow for the six months: -$65.8M (vs. -$50.3M, 31% worse). That is about $11M a month, or about $33M a quarter.
- Derived runway: $117.3M / $11.0M a month is about 10.7 months, or about 11.8 months on operating cash flow alone. This assumes the H1 burn rate holds, ignores further draws on the facility and any debt service, and counts restricted cash. Treat it as a rough guide. The release flags significant dilution risk from any note exchange or warrant exercise, and refinancing of the convertible notes remains an open issue.

## Guidance and cost actions

- Q3 2025 net revenue outlook: $68M-$73M. The midpoint of $70.5M is 6% below Q2 (derived), and the range is 3% to 9% below. There is no full-year guidance. The release cites "elevated uncertainty".
- Reduction in force, approved Aug 6: about 44 North America employees, about 6% of global staff. The implied global headcount is about 730 (derived).
  - One-time cash cost: $0.8M-$1.3M, mostly in Q3.
  - Savings over twelve months: $5.0M-$6.0M cash compensation, plus $0.5M-$1.0M non-cash stock-based compensation.
  - Derived: net first-year cash benefit is about $3.7M-$5.2M, and payback is about 2-3 months. The cash savings are only about 4% of the annualized burn.
- Management is accelerating its transformation plan. It is cutting operating expenses faster to fit near-term revenue, prioritizing distribution of core products, and investing in margin expansion.
- AlixPartners' role is expanded. John Boken, an AlixPartners partner, is interim Chief Transformation Officer.

## Takeaways for the review

1. Revenue is falling faster than costs. U.S. retail and international QSR burger demand are the weak spots, and the Q3 guide implies another sequential decline.
2. The net loss improvement is FX-driven and low quality. Operating loss, gross margin and EBITDA margin all worsened.
3. Liquidity is thin against about $1.2B of debt. Runway is roughly a year at the current burn rate, and the dilution and refinancing risks stay central to the thesis.
