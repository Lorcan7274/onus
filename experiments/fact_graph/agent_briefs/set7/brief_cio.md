**Subject: Beyond Meat (BYND) Q2 2025: volume decline, wider losses, thin liquidity against $1.2B of debt**

Hi,

Beyond Meat reported Q2 (quarter ended June 28, 2025) on August 6. Demand is still shrinking, margins are weaker, and liquidity is small next to the debt load. Management's response is more cost cutting and a turnaround adviser, not a recovery.

**Key results (Q2 2025 vs. Q2 2024)**

| Metric | Q2 2025 | Q2 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Net revenues | $75.0M | $93.2M |
| Gross profit / margin | $8.6M / 11.5% | $13.7M / 14.7% |
| Operating expenses | $47.4M | $47.6M |
| Operating loss / margin | $(38.8)M / -51.8% | $(33.9)M / -36.4% |
| Net loss / per share | $(33.2)M / $(0.43) | $(34.5)M / $(0.53) |
| Adjusted EBITDA / margin | $(26.0)M / -34.7% | $(23.0)M / -24.7% |

**Drivers**

Revenue fell 19.6%, driven by an 18.9% drop in volume and a 0.9% drop in net revenue per pound. The weakness was concentrated in:

- **U.S. retail:** down 26.7% to $32.9M, on weak category demand, reduced distribution points and higher trade discounts.
- **International foodservice:** down 25.8%, on lost burger sales to certain QSR customers.
- **International retail:** down 9.8%.
- **U.S. foodservice:** up 6.8% to $11.1M, the only growing channel.

The lower net loss is not an operating improvement. Total other income of $5.7M, mostly foreign-currency gains, offset the larger operating loss.

**Margins**

Gross margin fell 320 bps. It included $1.7M of China wind-down costs, plus higher inventory provisions and materials costs on lower volume. Operating expenses were flat year over year but included $7.5M of charges: $4.5M non-routine SG&A, $2.5M arbitration legal costs and $0.5M lease termination.

**Balance sheet and cash burn**

Cash, including restricted cash, was $117.3M against $1.2B of debt. Operating cash use for the first half was $59.4M, up from $47.8M. Capex was $6.4M. Financing inflow of $33.6M included an initial $40.0M draw on the Unprocessed Foods delayed-draw term loan. Without that draw, cash would be materially lower.

**Guidance**

There is no full-year guidance. The only outlook is Q3 revenue of $68M to $73M. Management also announced:

- A reduction in force of about 44 North American employees, roughly 6% of the workforce.
- John Boken of AlixPartners as interim Chief Transformation Officer.
- A stated goal of strengthening the balance sheet, with no specifics.

**My calculations**

- **Normalized results:** Adding back the disclosed charges, gross margin is about 13.7% ((8.6+1.7)/75.0) and operating loss is about $29.6M. Normalized operating expenses are about $39.9M, 16% below last year's reported $47.6M. The release does not say whether the prior year had similar items.
- **Cash burn:** First-half operating cash use plus capex is $65.8M, about $11M a month. At that pace, $117.3M of cash lasts roughly 11 months. That is before the RIF savings and before any Q3 revenue decline.
- **Leverage:** Cash covers about 10% of debt. Net debt is roughly $1.08B.
- **Q3 guide:** The $70.5M midpoint is about 6% below Q2, so the decline continues.
- **RIF:** Cash savings of $5–6M over twelve months against $0.8–1.3M of one-time costs pays back within a few months. It is small relative to the roughly $59M first-half operating cash burn.
- **Concentration:** U.S. retail is 44% of revenue and shrinking fastest.

**Implications**

Cost cuts alone cannot fix this. Gross profit of $8.6M covers only about a fifth of operating expenses. The investment case depends on a balance sheet restructuring, which the risk language says could mean significant equity dilution through note exchanges and lender warrants. The next catalysts are Q3 revenue against the $68M–$73M range, further financing or note actions, and the pace of operating expense reductions. I would not add exposure ahead of clarity on the capital structure.
