# Beyond Meat (BYND): Q2 2025 Morning Meeting Brief

Source: the Beyond Meat Q2 2025 earnings release dated August 6, 2025 (quarter ended June 28, 2025), narrative text only. The release's financial statement tables, its channel and volume tables, and its non-GAAP reconciliation tables are not in the source file. The deck uses no market prices, consensus figures or other sources. Dollar figures are in US$ millions unless stated otherwise. Where a figure is "derived", it is our arithmetic on the reported rounded figures, not a company disclosure.

---

## Slide 1: The headline

- **Revenue fell 19.6% from the year-ago quarter to $75.0M.** Volume fell 18.9% and net revenue per pound fell 0.9%.
- **Margins got worse at every level.** Gross margin was 11.5% (14.7% a year ago), operating margin -51.8% (-36.4%) and Adjusted EBITDA margin -34.7% (-24.7%).
- **Net loss narrowed slightly to $33.2M ($0.43 per share), from $34.5M ($0.53).** The only reason is a $5.7M other-income line, mostly foreign currency gains. Operating loss widened by $4.9M.
- **Q3 2025 revenue guide is $68-73M**, down again from Q2. There is no full-year guidance.
- **The balance sheet is the main issue.** Cash including restricted cash was $117.3M against about $1.2B of debt. H1 operating cash burn was $59.4M. A $40M first draw on the Unprocessed Foods delayed-draw term loan kept cash topped up.
- **Management response:** cutting about 44 jobs (around 6% of the global workforce), naming an AlixPartners turnaround partner as interim Chief Transformation Officer, and "strengthening our balance sheet for the longer term."
- CEO's description of the quarter: "disappointed". Weak category demand, especially in US retail and some international foodservice markets.

- What drove revenue: volume down 18.9%, net revenue per pound down 0.9%. Price per pound fell because of higher trade discounts and product mix, partly offset by favorable FX and some price increases.
- What drove gross margin: cost of goods sold per pound rose (higher inventory provision and materials costs, lower volume), plus $1.7M of China exit costs in COGS.
- The $5.7M of other income came mainly from realized and unrealized foreign currency gains. It should not be treated as recurring.

- **US retail is the biggest problem.** Weak category demand and lost points of distribution. Price per pound fell because of higher trade discounts. Ingredient sales were $0.1M, versus $0.8M a year ago.
- **US foodservice is the only channel that grew.** Price increases and mix helped, and ground beef and dinner sausage volumes rose.
- **International retail:** lower sales in Canada (burger, dinner sausage, ground beef) and of burgers in the EU. Favorable FX and mix lifted price per pound.
- **International foodservice:** lower burger sales to some QSR customers. Price per pound fell on product mix.
- Note: the four Q2 2024 channel figures add to $93.3M against the $93.2M reported total, a rounding difference. Year-ago shares and subtotals use the channel sum.

## Slide 4: One-time charges and cost actions

**Q2 2025 charges named in the release**

| Item | Where recorded | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Suspending and largely ending operations in China | Cost of goods sold | $1.7M |
| Certain non-routine SG&A | Operating expenses | $4.5M |
| Extra arbitration legal costs (dispute with a former co-manufacturer) | Operating expenses | $2.5M |
| Partial lease termination at the El Segundo campus headquarters | Operating expenses | $0.5M |
| **Total** | | **$9.2M** (matches the release's aggregate figure for net loss) |

- Without these items, operating expenses fell year over year, mainly on lower marketing and selling spend.
- **Workforce cut (approved August 6, 2025):** about 44 North America roles, roughly 6% of the global workforce.
  - One-time cash charges of $0.8-1.3M, mostly in Q3 2025. The timing could slip because of legal requirements.
  - Expected savings over the next 12 months: $5.0-6.0M in cash compensation, plus $0.5-1.0M of non-cash stock compensation.
- **Leadership:** John Boken, a Partner and Managing Director in AlixPartners' Turnaround and Restructuring practice, is interim Chief Transformation Officer. The company is also expanding its AlixPartners engagement.
- **Stated priorities:** cut operating expenses faster to fit expected near-term revenue, focus distribution on core product lines, and invest in margin improvement on those lines.

## Slide 5: Cash and funding

| Item (six months to date) | H1 2025 | H1 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Cash from operating activities | $(59.4)M | $(47.8)M |
| Capital expenditures | $(6.4)M | $(2.5)M |
| Cash from investing activities | $(6.1)M | +$1.2M |
| Cash from financing activities | +$33.6M | $(1.0)M |
| Free cash flow (operating cash flow minus capex, derived) | $(65.8)M | $(50.3)M |

- **At June 28, 2025:** cash including restricted cash was $117.3M, and total debt outstanding was about $1.2B.
- **New funding:** a $40.0M first draw on the delayed-draw term loan from Unprocessed Foods, LLC, an affiliate of Ahimsa Foundation. The loan agreement is dated May 7, 2025. Debt issuance costs partly offset the draw within financing cash flow.
- **Dilution risk:** the lenders on that loan hold warrants for common stock. The release also flags "significant dilution" if any convertible notes are exchanged for equity.
- **Other risks named in the forward-looking statements:** whether cash is enough to meet liquidity needs; the ability to refinance the convertible notes or repurchase them for cash; possible tax from cancellation-of-indebtedness income in a note exchange; the effect on net operating losses; and access to the "at the market" equity program.
- Management says it continues to pursue its goal of "strengthening our balance sheet for the longer term."

## Slide 6: Outlook

- **Q3 2025 guidance:** net revenues of **$68-73M**, with a midpoint of $70.5M.
  - That is 2.7% to 9.3% below Q2 2025's $75.0M (derived), or about 6.0% lower at the midpoint.
  - The year-ago Q3 figure is not in the source, so no year-over-year comparison is shown.
- **No full-year 2025 guidance.** Management cites "an elevated level of uncertainty within its operating environment."
- **No guidance on margins, EBITDA or cash burn.**
- **What management is working on:**
  - Faster operating-expense cuts.
  - Wider distribution for core product lines.
  - Margin improvement work on core products.
  - Balance sheet work.
- **Expected near-term items:**
  - Q3 cash charges from the workforce cut.
  - Further steps on the excess space at the campus headquarters.
  - Completing the China exit.
  - The co-manufacturer arbitration, which will keep generating legal costs.

## Slide 8: Talking points and what to watch

- **Demand is the core issue.** Volume fell 18.9%, and US retail lost distribution as well as velocity. The Q3 guide implies revenue keeps falling from Q2.
- **The quality of the earnings beat on net loss is low.** The narrower net loss came from FX gains. On an operating basis the loss widened.
- **Cost cuts are small next to the cash burn.** The workforce cut saves about $5-6M a year against about $66M of H1 free cash flow burn. The bigger opex lever is ending one-time charges and continued marketing and selling cuts.
- **The capital structure is the main event.** About $1.2B of debt against $117M of cash. Things to watch: further delayed-draw term loan draws, any convertible note exchange or refinancing (with dilution and tax implications), warrant exercise and use of the "at the market" program.
- **Questions for the call or filings:**
  - Gross margin path once the China costs and inventory provisions roll off.
  - Q3 cash burn.
  - How much remains available on the delayed-draw term loan.
  - Timing of the convertible note plan.
  - Distribution trends in US retail.
- **Source caveats:**
  - The channel volume tables, the charges table and the non-GAAP reconciliations are not in the source.
  - Restricted cash is not split out from cash.
  - The release date is August 6, 2025. Nothing after that date is reflected here.
