# Beyond Meat (BYND) Q2 2025 Earnings-Call Prep Sheet

Source: Q2 2025 earnings release dated Aug 6, 2025 (quarter ended June 28, 2025). The source file is narrative only, and its channel, volume and charge tables are not included, so channel figures come from the text. "Derived" marks figures I calculated. Treat derived figures as approximate because of rounding. Call: Aug 6, 5:00 p.m. ET.

## Summary

Revenue fell 19.6% to $75.0M, driven by an 18.9% drop in volume and a 0.9% drop in net revenue per pound. U.S. retail (-26.7%) and international foodservice (-25.8%) were the weak spots, and only U.S. foodservice grew. Gross margin fell to 11.5% from 14.7%, including $1.7M of China wind-down costs. Operating expenses were flat at $47.4M, but $7.5M of that was non-routine items. Operating loss widened to $38.8M (-51.8% margin) and Adjusted EBITDA loss widened to $26.0M (-34.7%). Net loss narrowed to $33.2M only because of $5.7M of other income, mainly FX gains. Cash is $117.3M against $1.2B of debt. Six-month operating cash burn was $59.4M, up from $47.8M, and a $40M delayed-draw loan draw helped fund it. Management is not giving full-year guidance. It guided Q3 revenue to $68-73M, which is below Q2. It announced a 44-person RIF (about 6% of the workforce), an expanded AlixPartners relationship and an interim Chief Transformation Officer, John Boken. The questions below focus on whether the decline is stabilizing, how credible the margin and cost plan is, and how long the liquidity lasts.

## Questions for management

**1. Is the volume decline bottoming?**
- Numbers: Revenue was down 19.6%, and volume was down 18.9% against a 0.9% price decline, so volume explains almost all of the drop. U.S. retail revenue fell $12.0M, or about 66% of the $18.2M total decline (derived). U.S. retail volume was down 24.2%, and its share of revenue slipped to 43.9% from 48.2% (derived).
- Question: How much of the U.S. retail volume loss comes from reduced points of distribution rather than weak category demand? What do scanner data and weekly trends show for velocity per point of distribution, and are lost points recoverable?

**2. What does the Q3 guide imply, and why no full-year view?**
- Numbers: Q3 revenue is guided to $68-73M, a midpoint of $70.5M. That is 6.0% below Q2's $75.0M, a range of -9.3% to -2.7% sequentially (derived). The year-ago Q3 base is not in this release.
- Question: What are the assumptions behind the range, including distribution losses, trade spend, QSR burger orders and the China exit? Does the low end assume further account losses? When will management feel able to give annual guidance again?

**3. U.S. retail pricing: how much is promotion?**
- Numbers: U.S. retail net revenue per pound fell 3.2% on higher trade discounts, while the total company figure fell 0.9%. U.S. foodservice price/mix rose 4.4%. Ingredient sales in U.S. retail fell to $0.1M from $0.8M, so U.S. retail ex-ingredients fell about 25.6% (derived).
- Question: Is deeper trade spending buying volume, or just defending shelf space? What is the plan for the pricing architecture, and how does that interact with the "increased distribution of core product lines" priority?

**4. International foodservice and the QSR dependence**
- Numbers: International foodservice revenue fell 25.8% to $15.1M, with volume down 21.6%, on lower burger sales to certain QSR customers. Total international revenue fell 18.4% to $31.0M (derived) and is 41.3% of the total (derived). International retail fell 9.8%, with volume down 13.1% in Canada and the EU.
- Question: Are the lost QSR volumes permanent or timing-related, and is there a replacement pipeline? How many customers drive this decline, and is more concentration risk coming?

**5. Gross margin: what is the path from 11.5%?**
- Numbers: Gross margin was 11.5% against 14.7% (-3.2 pts). Excluding the $1.7M China charge, gross profit would be about $10.3M and margin about 13.7% (derived). COGS per pound rose on a higher inventory provision and higher materials cost, partly offset by lower manufacturing and logistics costs.
- Question: How much of the underlying margin pressure is deleverage on lower volume and how much is inventory provisions and materials? Which "margin expansion initiatives" in the core products will land first, and how large are they? Are tariffs a factor?

**6. Quality of the opex discipline**
- Numbers: Opex was flat at $47.4M, but it included $7.5M of non-routine items (derived): $4.5M non-routine SG&A, $2.5M arbitration legal fees and $0.5M lease termination. Excluding those, opex was about $39.9M, which is 16.2% below last year's $47.6M if the year-ago quarter had no comparable items (derived, unverified). Opex was 63.2% of revenue against 51.1% (derived). The RIF saves $5.0-6.0M of cash compensation over twelve months, about 2.9% of annualized Q2 opex of about $190M (derived), at a one-time cost of $0.8-1.3M.
- Question: What is the target quarterly opex run rate, and by when? What are the "non-routine SG&A" items and will they recur? What is the arbitration exposure and timing? Is the RIF enough, or is more coming given "rapidly and aggressively reducing" expenses?

**7. Earnings quality: other income and adjusted metrics**
- Numbers: Operating loss was $38.8M and net loss was $33.2M, a $5.6M gap that comes from $5.7M of other income, mostly FX gains (derived). Special items totaled $9.2M ($1.7M + $4.5M + $2.5M + $0.5M), so the adjusted operating loss is about $29.6M, or about -39.5% of revenue (derived). Adjusted EBITDA loss widened 13.0% even so.
- Question: How much of the FX gain is recurring, and what drives it? On an adjusted basis, why did losses widen if costs are being cut? What should we model for Q3 charges beyond the RIF?

**8. Cash burn and runway**
- Numbers: Six-month operating cash burn was $59.4M, up 24.3%, plus $6.4M of capex, so about $65.8M of burn, or about $11.0M a month (derived). Cash of $117.3M therefore covers about 10-11 months at that rate (derived, simple division). That balance includes the $40M initial draw on the Unprocessed Foods delayed-draw term loan, net of issuance costs. Financing inflow was $33.6M.
- Question: What is the expected cash burn for the second half, and when does it improve? Why did capex rise 2.6x while the company is shrinking? How much more can be drawn on the delayed-draw facility, and what conditions apply? What minimum cash does management need to run the business?

**9. Capital structure: $1.2B of debt against $117M of cash**
- Numbers: Total debt was $1.2B against cash of $117.3M (cash is about 9.8% of debt, derived). Net loss per share of $0.43 against $0.53 implies about 77M weighted shares against about 65M, roughly 19% more (derived). The release flags dilution from note exchanges and from the lender's warrants, plus the convertible notes, the ATM program and the ability to refinance.
- Question: What is the plan for the convertible notes, and how are talks with holders going? How much more dilution should investors expect from further note exchanges, warrant exercise and the ATM? Does the AlixPartners work include a restructuring scenario? Does the Chief Transformation Officer appointment signal a shift towards restructuring?

**10. Execution risk: transformation, leadership and China**
- Numbers: The RIF is 44 employees, about 6% of the global workforce, which implies a workforce of roughly 730 (derived). It follows a China wind-down that cost $1.7M in COGS this quarter, and a partial lease termination at the El Segundo headquarters ($0.5M, with more excess space to sublet or exit). An interim Chief Transformation Officer from AlixPartners has just joined.
- Question: What does the CTO own, and what milestones will be reported externally? Are there other product lines, plants, co-manufacturing agreements or geographies for exit? How much more is there to come on the headquarters lease? Are there any further cuts to the 2025 commercial plans, including new product launches (Beyond Ground, Steak Filet, Chicken Pieces)?
