# Beyond Meat (BYND): Q2 2025 Bull vs. Bear

*Source: Q2 2025 earnings release dated August 6, 2025 (quarter ended June 28, 2025). Derived figures are our own calculations from the release, in $M unless noted.*

## The quarter in brief

Net revenues fell 19.6% to $75.0M. Volume was down 18.9% and net revenue per pound down 0.9%, with weakness concentrated in U.S. retail and international QSR burgers. Gross margin fell 320bp to 11.5%. That includes $1.7M of China-exit costs. Operating loss widened to $38.8M, and Adjusted EBITDA loss widened to $26.0M (-34.7% of revenue). Net loss improved slightly to $33.2M, but only because of a $6.3M swing in other income, mostly FX gains. Management is cutting about 6% of the workforce, has brought in an AlixPartners interim Chief Transformation Officer, and guided Q3 revenue to $68–73M. Cash was $117.3M (including restricted) against $1.2B of debt.

## Bull case

1. **The cost base is coming down underneath the one-off charges.** Excluding $7.5M of items (non-routine SG&A $4.5M, arbitration legal $2.5M, lease termination $0.5M), opex was about $39.9M, down 16% from $47.6M, driven by lower marketing and selling spend. The RIF adds $5–6M of annual cash savings, for a one-time cost of $0.8–1.3M (payback of about 2–3 months). Hiring a restructuring specialist as interim CTO points to further cuts.
2. **Underlying gross margin held up better than the headline.** Excluding China costs, gross margin was about 13.7%, only about 100bp lower despite an 18.9% volume drop. Manufacturing costs (including depreciation) and logistics costs per pound fell. The China exit removes a drag going forward.
3. **U.S. foodservice is growing, with pricing power.** Revenue rose 6.8%, with net revenue per pound up 4.4% and volume up 2.3% on ground beef and dinner sausage. Its share of the mix rose to 14.8% from 11.2%. Pricing was also positive in international retail (+3.9% per pound).
4. **The business is diversified beyond the weakest channel.** International was 41.3% of revenue (vs. 40.8%). International retail fell only 9.8%, far less than U.S. retail's 26.7%.
5. **Lenders are still providing capital, and losses are per-share smaller.** The $40M first draw on the Unprocessed Foods delayed-draw term loan shows funding is available. Net loss per share improved 19% ($0.43 vs. $0.53), and management says it is still working to strengthen the balance sheet.

## Bear case

1. **Demand keeps shrinking, and guidance says it will continue.** Volume fell 18.9%. U.S. retail alone was $12.0M of the $18.2M revenue decline (about 66%), driven by weak category demand, lost distribution and heavier trade discounts. U.S. retail's share of the mix fell to 43.9% from 48.2%. The Q3 guide of $68–73M implies a further 3–9% sequential decline (midpoint -6%).
2. **Margins are moving the wrong way.** Cost of goods sold per pound rose on higher inventory provisions and higher materials costs. Operating margin fell 1,540bp to -51.8%, and Adjusted EBITDA margin fell 1,000bp to -34.7%. At about 13.7% underlying gross margin, covering roughly $39.9M of quarterly underlying opex would take about $290M of quarterly revenue, nearly 4x the current level.
3. **The balance sheet is the overriding issue.** Debt of $1.2B is about 10x cash and about 4x annualized revenue (about $300M). H1 free cash flow was about -$65.8M ($59.4M operating burn plus $6.4M capex), or about $33M per quarter. That gives about 3.5 quarters of runway on $117.3M, and the cash figure includes restricted cash and $40M of borrowed money.
4. **The lower net loss is low quality, and dilution is building.** The improvement came from a $6.3M swing in other income, mostly FX gains. Operating loss was actually $4.9M worse. Implied weighted shares are about 77M vs. about 65M (about +19%). The release flags significant dilution risk from any exchange of the convertible notes and from the lender's warrants.
5. **The cost cuts are small relative to the losses.** The RIF's $5–6M of savings is only about 5% of the annualized Adjusted EBITDA loss (about $104M). The "one-time" charges keep coming (China, arbitration, headquarters lease), and capex rose 2.6x while the business is burning cash.

## What would change our view

- **Becoming more constructive:** Q3 revenue at or above $73M with U.S. retail distribution stabilizing. Gross margin back above 15% excluding charges. Quarterly operating cash burn below about $20M. A restructuring of the convertible notes that extends maturities with limited dilution.
- **Becoming more negative:** Q3 revenue below $68M. Further distribution losses or deeper trade discounting. Another quarter with about $30M+ of burn without new financing, which would put runway under a year. Continued losses of international QSR volume.

**Net:** The evidence leans bear. There is real progress on opex and in U.S. foodservice, but it is outweighed by double-digit volume declines, shrinking margins and a capital structure carrying about $1.2B of debt against about 3.5 quarters of cash.
