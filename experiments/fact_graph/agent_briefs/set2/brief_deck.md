# Beyond Meat (NASDAQ: BYND) - Q2 2025 Client Briefing

Source: company earnings release dated August 6, 2025 (quarter ended June 28, 2025). "Derived" figures are our own calculations from release numbers.

## Slide 1: Q2 2025 at a glance

- Net revenues of $75.0M, down 19.6% year-over-year; volume fell 18.9% and net revenue per pound fell 0.9%.
- Gross margin of 11.5% (14.7% a year ago), including $1.7M of China wind-down costs.
- Operating loss widened to $38.8M (-51.8% margin) from $33.9M (-36.4%); Adjusted EBITDA loss of $26.0M (-34.7%).
- Net loss narrowed to $33.2M ($0.43/share) only because other income, net, swung to +$5.7M from -$0.6M, mostly FX gains.
- Management called the results disappointing and cited category softness, mainly in U.S. retail and international foodservice.

- Operating expenses were flat despite revenue falling nearly a fifth, so operating deleverage drove the margin damage.

## Slide 3: Channel performance

- U.S. retail was $32.9M, down 26.7% (volume -24.2%, price/mix -3.2%), on weak category demand, reduced points of distribution and higher trade discounts.
- U.S. foodservice was the only growth channel at $11.1M, up 6.8% (volume +2.3%, price/mix +4.4%), led by ground beef and dinner sausage.
- International retail was $15.9M, down 9.8% (volume -13.1%, price/mix +3.9% helped by FX), with weakness in Canada and EU burgers.
- International foodservice was $15.1M, down 25.8% (volume -21.6%), on lower burger sales to certain QSR customers.
- Mix (derived): U.S. retail fell to 43.9% of revenue from 48.2%; international rose to 41.3% from 40.8%.

## Slide 4: Cost structure and restructuring

- Q2 included $4.5M of non-routine SG&A, $2.5M of incremental arbitration legal costs (former co-manufacturer dispute) and $0.5M of partial HQ lease termination costs.
- Gross margin was also hurt by higher inventory provision and materials costs per pound, partly offset by lower manufacturing and logistics expense.
- Excluding the items above, opex fell on lower marketing and selling spend; management says it will cut opex "more rapidly and aggressively".
- Reduction in force of about 44 North American employees (about 6% of global headcount), with a one-time cash cost of $0.8M-$1.3M, mostly in Q3.
- Expected savings of $5.0M-$6.0M cash compensation over 12 months plus $0.5M-$1.0M non-cash; AlixPartners relationship expanded, with John Boken as interim Chief Transformation Officer.

## Slide 5: Cash and funding

- Cash, including restricted cash, was $117.3M and total debt about $1.2B at June 28, 2025.
- Six-month operating cash burn was $59.4M versus $47.8M a year ago; capex was $6.4M versus $2.5M.
- Financing inflow of $33.6M in the first half included an initial $40.0M draw on the Delayed Draw Term Loan Facility with Unprocessed Foods, LLC, net of issuance costs.
- Release flags dilution risk from convertible note exchanges and lender warrants, refinancing risk on the Notes, and reliance on the at-the-market program.
- Management says it still aims to strengthen the balance sheet for the longer term; no specific transaction is detailed in the release.

## Slide 6: Outlook

- No full-year guidance; management cites elevated uncertainty and limits its outlook to Q3 2025.
- Q3 2025 net revenues are expected at $68M-$73M.
- Midpoint of $70.5M implies roughly -6.0% sequentially from Q2's $75.0M (range -9.3% to -2.7%, derived).
- Stated priorities: cut opex to fit near-term revenue, push distribution of core product lines, and invest in margin expansion.
- The release gives no Q3 margin, EBITDA or cash guidance, so the earnings path remains unanchored.

## Slide 7: So what - our derived figures

- Revenue decline of $18.2M: U.S. retail alone accounts for $12.0M, or about 66%; international foodservice adds $5.3M.
- Ex-China gross margin would have been about 13.7% ($10.3M on $75.0M), still 1.0 pt below last year, so the margin problem is not only one-off.
- Opex excluding the three disclosed items was about $39.9M, or roughly 53% of revenue; operating loss ex-items was about $29.6M (-39.5% margin) versus the reported -51.8%.
- Net loss was flattered by $5.7M of other income; the operating loss before it was $38.8M, 17% larger than the $33.2M net loss.
- Cash math: first-half operating cash flow less capex was about -$65.8M (about -$32.9M per quarter). Cash of $117.3M covers about 3.6 quarters at that rate, and cash is only about 10% of the $1.2B debt (derived, before any new funding).
- RIF savings of about $5.5M (midpoint) equal only about 3% of the annualized Q2 opex run-rate of about $190M, so further cuts are needed.

## Slide 8: Takeaways and questions for the meeting

- Thesis: the top line is still shrinking at a high-teens rate, U.S. retail is the swing factor, and cost cuts to date have not offset operating deleverage.
- Positives to monitor: U.S. foodservice growth, international retail pricing, a narrowing net loss and the transformation team.
- Watch items: Q3 revenue against the $68M-$73M range, gross margin after China costs roll off, opex run-rate after the RIF, and the pace of cash burn.
- Balance-sheet risk is central: about 3.6 quarters of runway at the first-half rate, a $1.2B debt load, and dilution from any note exchange or warrants.
- Discussion: how much of the retail decline is category softness versus lost distribution, and what funding or restructuring path is most plausible.
