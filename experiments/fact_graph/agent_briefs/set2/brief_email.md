**Subject:** BYND Q2 2025 review and position recommendation

**To:** Investment Committee
**From:** Buy-side analyst

Team,

Below is my review of Beyond Meat's Q2 2025 results (quarter ended June 28, 2025, released August 6, 2025). This release is the only source used. Reported figures are as stated by the company; ratios and changes are my calculations.

**Summary**

Q2 was weak on nearly every measure. Revenue fell 19.6% to $75.0M, and the Adjusted EBITDA loss widened to $26.0M from $23.0M. I recommend we keep the current small position at its current size, do not add, and set explicit exit triggers. This release is from August 2025, so any later filings should be reviewed before we act.

**Results versus the year-ago quarter**

Revenue was $75.0M versus $93.2M. Volume fell 18.9% and net revenue per pound fell 0.9%. Gross profit was $8.6M (11.5% margin) versus $13.7M (14.7%). Excluding the $1.7M China charge, the gross margin would have been about 13.7%. Loss from operations widened to $38.8M from $33.9M, and the operating margin was -51.8% versus -36.4%. Operating expenses were flat at $47.4M, but excluding $7.5M of called-out items they fell to about $39.9M, roughly 16% lower. Net loss narrowed to $33.2M ($0.43 per share) from $34.5M, but only because other income of $5.7M, mostly FX gains, replaced a $0.6M expense.

**What drove it**

U.S. retail is the main problem. Revenue there fell 26.7% to $32.9M on a 24.2% volume decline, driven by weak category demand and fewer points of distribution. It is now 44% of sales versus 48% a year ago. International foodservice fell 25.8%, mainly on lower QSR burger volume. U.S. foodservice was the only growing channel, up 6.8%. Gross margin was pressured by higher inventory provisions and materials costs, partly offset by lower manufacturing and logistics costs.

**Balance sheet and cash burn**

Cash including restricted cash was $117.3M against $1.2B of debt, implying net debt of about $1.08B. Operating cash burn for the first half was $59.4M (versus $47.8M a year earlier), and capex was $6.4M (versus $2.5M). Free cash flow was therefore about -$65.8M for H1, or roughly -$11M per month. At that pace, the cash balance covers about 11 months. That runway uses restricted cash, which may not be fully available. The release also flags possible dilution from note exchanges and lender warrants. Standalone Q2 cash flow is not disclosed.

**Guidance and cost actions**

The company is not giving full-year guidance. Q3 revenue is guided to $68M to $73M, a midpoint of $70.5M, about 6% below Q2. The reduction in force removes about 44 North American roles (about 6% of the global workforce, or roughly 730 people in total). It is expected to save $5.0M to $6.0M in cash compensation annually for one-time charges of $0.8M to $1.3M, a payback of about two to three months. Against an annualized Adjusted EBITDA loss of about $104M ($26.0M x 4), those savings are small. Management also appointed an interim Chief Transformation Officer from AlixPartners, which signals a turnaround.

**Recommendation**

Keep the position at its current small size and do not add. Revenue is still falling, margins are compressing, and the cash runway is under a year at H1 burn. I would exit if any of the following occurs: (1) next quarter's revenue comes in below the $68M low end of the Q3 guide; (2) cash falls toward $60M without new financing; or (3) a dilutive note exchange or equity raise is announced. I would revisit adding only after two quarters of stable volume and a clear path to positive gross margin on a funded balance sheet.

Happy to walk through the workings.
