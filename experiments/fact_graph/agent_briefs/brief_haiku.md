# Beyond Meat (BYND): Q2 2025 Earnings Brief

**Source:** Q2 2025 earnings release dated August 6, 2025 (quarter ended June 28, 2025). Derived figures are our calculations.

## Headline

Weak quarter driven by volume. Net revenue fell 19.6% to $75.0M on an 18.9% volume decline, and the operating loss widened to $38.8M (-51.8% margin). The net loss narrowed to $33.2M only because of $5.7M of non-operating FX gains. Adjusted EBITDA loss widened to $26.0M. Management guides Q3 revenue to $68-73M, gives no full-year guidance, and is cutting 44 roles (about 6% of the workforce). Cash was $117.3M against $1.2B of debt.

## Key numbers

| Metric | Q2 2025 | Year-ago (Q2 2024) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net revenues | $75.0M | $93.2M | -19.6% |
| Gross profit | $8.6M | $13.7M | -37.2% |
| Gross margin | 11.5% | 14.7% | -3.2 pts |
| Operating expenses | $47.4M | $47.6M | -0.4% |
| Loss from operations | ($38.8M) | ($33.9M) | 14.5% wider |
| Operating margin | -51.8% | -36.4% | -15.4 pts |
| Net loss | ($33.2M) | ($34.5M) | 3.8% narrower |
| EPS | ($0.43) | ($0.53) | Narrower |
| Adjusted EBITDA | ($26.0M) | ($23.0M) | 13.0% wider |
| Adj. EBITDA margin | -34.7% | -24.7% | -10.0 pts |

**Gross profit and margin.** Both include $1.7M of China wind-down costs. Excluding them, gross margin is about 13.7% (down 1.0 pt, not 3.2 pts). Cost of goods per pound rose on lower volume, higher inventory provisions and higher materials costs, partly offset by lower manufacturing and logistics costs.

**Operating expenses.** The $47.4M includes $7.5M of non-routine items: $4.5M SG&A, $2.5M arbitration legal fees and $0.5M lease termination costs. Excluding these, opex is $39.9M, down about 16% and driven by lower marketing and selling spend. The release does not say whether the year-ago quarter had comparable items, so this comparison assumes it did not.

## Channel trends

| Channel | Q2 2025 revenue | YoY | Volume | Revenue/lb | Share of revenue (year-ago) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| U.S. retail | $32.9M | -26.7% | -24.2% | -3.2% | 43.9% (48.2%) |
| U.S. foodservice | $11.1M | +6.8% | +2.3% | +4.4% | 14.8% (11.2%) |
| International retail | $15.9M | -9.8% | -13.1% | +3.9% | 21.2% (18.9%) |
| International foodservice | $15.1M | -25.8% | -21.6% | -5.3% | 20.1% (21.9%) |

U.S. retail accounts for about $12.0M of the roughly $18M revenue decline. The release cites weak category demand and fewer points of distribution there. Higher trade discounts also pressured price. U.S. foodservice was the only channel to grow, on both volume and price. Ingredient sales in U.S. retail fell from $0.8M to $0.1M.

## Cash and debt

- Cash, including restricted cash, was $117.3M at June 28, 2025. Total debt was $1.2B, so net debt was about $1.08B.
- Operating cash outflow for the first half was $59.4M, up 24% from $47.8M. Capex was $6.4M, up from $2.5M. First-half free cash flow was about -$65.8M, versus about -$50.3M a year earlier.
- Financing inflow of $33.6M includes an initial $40.0M draw on a delayed-draw term loan from Unprocessed Foods, LLC (an affiliate of Ahimsa Foundation), net of issuance costs.
- At the first-half burn rate (about $33M per quarter), the $117.3M balance covers roughly 3.5 quarters before further financing.
- The release does not split the $1.2B between the convertible notes and the Loan and Security Agreement. It warns of possible note exchanges, warrant exercises and dilution, and it references an at-the-market equity program.

## Guidance

- **Q3 2025 net revenue: $68M to $73M.** The midpoint of $70.5M implies a sequential decline of about 6% from $75.0M (range -2.7% to -9.3%).
- No full-year guidance, and no margin or earnings guidance.
- Workforce reduction: about 44 North American roles. One-time cash charges of $0.8M to $1.3M, mostly in Q3. Expected cash compensation savings of $5.0M to $6.0M over 12 months, plus $0.5M to $1.0M of non-cash savings.

## Our analysis

1. **The problem is volume, not price.** Volume fell 18.9% while revenue per pound fell only 0.9%. Price increases offset much of the pressure in foodservice and international retail. The U.S. retail distribution and demand problem is the biggest issue.
2. **Most of the gross margin decline is China.** The China costs explain about 2.2 of the 3.2-point drop. The remaining 1.0 pt reflects under-absorbed fixed costs on lower volume and higher inventory provisions.
3. **The net loss improvement is low quality.** Other income swung from a $0.6M expense to $5.7M of income, a $6.3M benefit from FX gains. Operating loss actually widened by $4.9M. Without the FX gain, the net loss would be about $39M.
4. **Adjusted EBITDA got worse even as core opex fell.** The $12.8M gap between operating loss and adjusted EBITDA is add-backs (likely D&A, stock compensation and other items). The reconciliation table is not in the source text, so we cannot break it down.
5. **Restructuring savings are small against the cost base.** The $5M to $6M annual savings is about $1.4M per quarter, roughly 3.5% of the $39.9M quarterly core opex. Reaching breakeven will require much more cost reduction, a volume recovery, or both.
6. **Dilution is building.** EPS implies roughly 77M weighted shares versus about 65M a year earlier, an increase of about 18% to 19%. This is approximate because EPS is rounded.

## Caveats

- This brief uses only the Q2 2025 release (August 6, 2025). Later results and events are not reflected.
- The source text drops the channel revenue table, the charges table and the non-GAAP reconciliations. Channel figures come from the narrative text.
