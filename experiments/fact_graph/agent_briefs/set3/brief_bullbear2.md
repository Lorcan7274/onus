# Beyond Meat (BYND): Q2 2025 Bull vs. Bear

*Source: Beyond Meat Q2 2025 earnings release (August 6, 2025) only. Derived figures are our calculations from release numbers.*

## Summary

Revenue fell 19.6% to $75.0M on an 18.9% volume decline, and gross margin dropped 3.2 points to 11.5%. Headline operating expenses were flat, but excluding $7.5M of non-routine items they fell about 16%. Adjusted EBITDA loss widened to $26.0M (-34.7% of revenue) from $23.0M. The reported net loss narrowed only because other income swung to a $5.7M gain, mostly FX. The bull case rests on cost cuts and U.S. foodservice growth. The bear case rests on volume loss, margin compression and a $1.2B debt load against $117.3M of cash. The Q3 revenue guide of $68M to $73M (midpoint $70.5M, about -6% sequentially) points to further decline. The release gives no Q3 2024 comparator, so we cannot derive a Q3 year-over-year figure.

## Bull case

1. **The cost reset is real and accelerating.** Operating expenses excluding $7.5M of non-routine items (SG&A, arbitration legal, lease termination) fell to $39.9M from $47.6M, led by lower marketing and selling. The reduction-in-force (about 44 North American roles, roughly 6% of the global workforce) targets $5.0M to $6.0M of annual cash savings plus $0.5M to $1.0M of non-cash savings. An interim Chief Transformation Officer was added.
2. **U.S. foodservice is the one growing channel.** Revenue rose 6.8% to $11.1M on 2.3% higher volume and 4.4% higher net revenue per pound. Its share of revenue rose to 14.8% from 11.2%, led by ground beef and dinner sausage.
3. **Pricing and mix held; volume is the problem.** Net revenue per pound fell only 0.9% while volume fell 18.9%. The demand and distribution issue is therefore concentrated in volume, not in per-unit economics.
4. **The underlying operating loss is smaller than reported.** Excluding $9.2M of China exit, non-routine SG&A, arbitration and lease charges, the operating loss would be about $29.6M, or -39.5% of revenue. The release does not restate the year-ago period on the same basis, so this is a like-for-like gap only for the current quarter. China operations are substantially ceased, which removes a loss-making cost base.

## Bear case

1. **Volume is falling, including distribution.** Volume fell 18.9%. U.S. retail volume fell 24.2% on weak category demand and fewer points of distribution. International foodservice volume fell 21.6% on lower QSR burger sales. Three of four channels shrank.
2. **Margins are moving the wrong way.** Gross margin fell 3.2 points to 11.5%. Excluding the $1.7M China charge it is about 13.7%, still roughly 1 point below last year. Cost of goods per pound rose on higher inventory provisions and materials costs. Adjusted EBITDA margin fell 10.0 points, and the adjusted loss grew $3.0M even after the opex reduction.
3. **Cost cuts are small against the revenue hole.** Gross profit fell $5.1M (-37%). The RIF's $5.0M to $6.0M of cash savings equals about 3% to 4% of annualized ex-item opex (about $160M). The program addresses the cost base at the margin but does not offset a 20% revenue decline.
4. **The net loss improvement is financial, not operational.** Other income, net swung from a $0.6M expense to a $5.7M gain, a $6.3M swing driven by FX transaction gains. Operating loss widened $4.9M. Removing the swing, the net loss would be roughly $39.5M against $34.5M a year ago (pre-tax approximation, ignoring tax).
5. **Leverage and dilution.** Total debt of $1.2B is about 10x the $117.3M of cash, a net debt of about $1.08B. Operating cash use for H1 was $59.4M, up 24% year over year. With $6.4M of capex, H1 free cash flow was about -$65.8M against about -$50.3M a year earlier. H1 financing included a $40.0M draw from Unprocessed Foods, an affiliate of the Ahimsa Foundation. Net loss divided by EPS implies roughly 77M weighted shares against 65M a year ago, about 19% more (derived from rounded EPS). The release flags possible note-exchange dilution and lender warrants.

## What would change our view

- **Turn more constructive if:** Q3 revenue lands at or above the $73M top of the guide (a sequential decline of 3% or less). Gross margin ex-China recovers to 14% or better. U.S. retail volume declines narrow well below -24% with distribution stabilizing. The RIF savings show up as a lower opex run-rate. The notes and loan are addressed without heavy equity dilution.
- **Turn more negative if:** Q3 revenue comes in below the $68M low end. Further impairment, inventory or lease charges appear. Cash burn runs above the H1 pace. Equity issuance or note exchanges accelerate. U.S. foodservice growth stalls, since it is the only growing channel.
