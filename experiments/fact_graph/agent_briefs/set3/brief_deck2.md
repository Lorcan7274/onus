# Beyond Meat (BYND): Q2 2025 Results Review

Internal research meeting: deck outline

Source: Beyond Meat revised Q2 2025 earnings release dated August 8, 2025 (quarter ended June 28, 2025). The revision only corrects SG&A and the line items that flow from it. The source file holds the narrative text only: the channel, volume, charges and financial-statement tables are not in it. All figures are in US$, and "derived" marks our own arithmetic on the rounded figures in the release.

---

## Slide 1: Key takeaways

- Revenue fell 19.6% year over year to $75.0M. Volume fell 18.9% and net revenue per pound fell 0.9%. Management blames weak plant-based category demand, lost U.S. retail distribution and lower burger sales to international QSR customers.
- Gross margin fell 320 bp to 11.5%. The operating loss widened slightly to $34.9M (-46.6% margin) even though operating expenses fell $4.1M.
- Net loss improved to $29.2M ($0.38/share) from $34.5M ($0.53/share). This came only from a $5.7M other-income gain, mostly FX. Adjusted EBITDA loss was $22.1M, a little better in dollars but worse as a share of revenue (-29.5% vs -24.7%).
- The balance sheet is the central issue. Cash including restricted cash is $117.3M against about $1.2B of debt. H1 operating cash burn was $59.4M, and liquidity relied on a $40.0M draw on the Unprocessed Foods delayed-draw term loan.
- Q3 revenue guidance of $68-73M implies a further sequential decline. There is still no full-year guidance. Management's response is a reduction-in-force (RIF), a wider AlixPartners engagement and an interim Chief Transformation Officer (CTO), John Boken.

- The improvement at the bottom line is non-operating. Without the $6.3M swing in other income, net loss would have been worse than a year ago.

## Slide 3: Channel performance

| Channel | Q2 2025 | Q2 2024 | Y/Y | Volume | Net rev/lb | Mix Q2'25 (derived) | Mix Q2'24 (derived) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| U.S. retail | $32.9M | $44.9M | -26.7% | -24.2% | -3.2% | 43.9% | 48.2% |
| U.S. foodservice | $11.1M | $10.4M | +6.8% | +2.3% | +4.4% | 14.8% | 11.2% |
| International retail | $15.9M | $17.6M | -9.8% | -13.1% | +3.9% | 21.2% | 18.9% |
| International foodservice | $15.1M | $20.4M | -25.8% | -21.6% | -5.3% | 20.1% | 21.9% |

- U.S. retail is the main problem. Revenue fell $12.0M, about two-thirds of the total decline, because of weak category demand, lost points of distribution and higher trade discounts. Ingredient sales fell to $0.1M from $0.8M, so U.S. retail excluding ingredients was down about 26% (derived).
- International foodservice fell $5.3M, about 29% of the decline. The cause was lower burger sales to certain QSR customers, plus a 5.3% fall in net revenue per pound from mix.
- U.S. foodservice is the only channel that grew (+$0.7M). Ground beef and dinner sausage drove it, along with price increases.
- International retail held up better (-9.8%). FX, mix and price lifted revenue per pound 3.9%, which partly offset lower volumes in Canada and fewer burger sales in the EU.
- Geography is unchanged: the U.S. is 58.7% of revenue vs 59.3% a year ago. The mix moved toward foodservice, which rose to 34.9% from 33.0% (derived).

## Slide 4: Costs and charges

- Gross profit includes $1.7M in cost of goods sold for the suspension and substantial cessation of China operations. Excluding it, gross margin would have been about 13.7% (derived), still roughly 100 bp below the year-ago 14.7%.
- Underlying COGS per pound rose because of lower volume, a higher inventory provision and higher materials costs. Lower manufacturing costs (including depreciation) and lower logistics costs partly offset this.
- Operating expenses of $43.5M include $3.6M of charges:
  - $2.5M of incremental arbitration-related legal expense in the dispute with a former co-manufacturer
  - $0.6M of non-routine SG&A
  - $0.5M for the partial lease termination at the El Segundo Campus Headquarters
- Excluding those charges, opex was about $39.9M, down about 16% y/y (derived; we do not know the year-ago items). The release attributes the decline mainly to lower marketing and selling spend.
- Total charges were $5.3M. Excluding them, the operating loss would have been about $29.6M (-39.5% margin) and the net loss about $23.9M (derived, before any tax effect).

## Slide 5: Cash, debt and funding

- At June 28, 2025: cash and cash equivalents including restricted cash of $117.3M; total outstanding debt of about $1.2B. Implied net debt is about $1.08B (derived).
- H1 2025 operating cash outflow was $59.4M vs $47.8M in H1 2024, about 24% higher. Capex rose to $6.4M from $2.5M.
- H1 investing activities used $6.1M, compared with $1.2M provided a year ago.
- H1 financing provided $33.6M. This includes the initial $40.0M draw on the delayed-draw term loan with Unprocessed Foods, LLC (an affiliate of Ahimsa Foundation, under the May 7, 2025 Loan and Security Agreement), net of issuance costs.
- Risk factors the release flags: refinancing or repurchasing the convertible notes, significant dilution from any note-for-equity exchange or from exercise of the lender warrants, cancellation-of-indebtedness tax exposure, and use of the at-the-market (ATM) program.

## Slide 6: Outlook and restructuring actions

- Q3 2025 net revenue guidance is $68-73M. The $70.5M midpoint is about 6% below Q2's $75.0M (range -2.7% to -9.3%, derived). There is no full-year guidance because of "elevated uncertainty".
- RIF approved on August 6: about 44 North America roles, roughly 6% of the global workforce.
  - One-time cash charges of $0.8-1.3M, mostly in Q3.
  - Expected 12-month savings of $5.0-6.0M cash compensation plus $0.5-1.0M non-cash stock-based compensation.
- John Boken of AlixPartners was appointed interim Chief Transformation Officer. The AlixPartners engagement has been widened to drive enterprise-wide transformation.
- Stated priorities:
  - cut opex faster to fit near-term revenue
  - prioritize distribution of core product lines
  - invest in margin expansion on core products
  - strengthen the balance sheet over the longer term

## Slide 8: Discussion points for the team

- Category versus company: how much of the 24.2% drop in U.S. retail volume is lost distribution that may be permanent, and how much is category demand?
- Is the international QSR burger shortfall customer-specific and lapping in coming quarters, or is it structural?
- Gross margin: can margin-expansion work and lower manufacturing costs offset deleverage at Q3 volumes, given the Q3 revenue guide of $68-73M?
- Capital structure: what is the timing and form of a convertible note solution, how much of the delayed-draw facility is still undrawn (not disclosed in the source), and what dilution comes from the exchange or the warrants?
- What would have to be true for the stock to work: a path to positive adjusted EBITDA and a credible financing plan before cash runs below about 2 quarters of burn.
